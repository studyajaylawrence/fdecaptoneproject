## 1. High-alert medication list

The following classes are designated high-alert at Contoso Health System:

- insulins, all routes and concentrations
- anticoagulants, including heparin, low-molecular-weight heparins and direct oral anticoagulants
- opioids, including patient-controlled analgesia
- concentrated electrolytes, including potassium chloride and hypertonic saline
- chemotherapy and other antineoplastic agents
- neuromuscular blocking agents
- insulin-like agents used for glycaemic emergencies, and intravenous glucose 50 percent

## 2. Independent double check

2.1 An **independent double check by two qualified staff** is required before administration of any
high-alert medication. The second checker must verify the order, the drug, the concentration, the
dose calculation, the pump settings and the patient identity **without prompting** from the first
checker.

2.2 Both checkers record their identity in the electronic medication record at the time of
administration.

2.3 Neuromuscular blocking agents are stored only in critical care, theatres and the emergency
department, in sealed containers labelled "Paralysing agent - causes respiratory arrest".

## 3. Patient identification

3.1 Two patient identifiers, the full name and the medical record number, are confirmed against
the wristband before every administration. The room or bed number is never an identifier.

3.2 Barcode scanning of patient and product is mandatory wherever barcode administration is
deployed. Bypassing a scan requires a documented reason and is reported weekly to the Medication
Safety Committee.

## 4. Infusion practice

4.1 All continuous intravenous insulin, heparin and opioid infusions run on a **smart pump with the
Contoso drug library engaged**. Manual rate entry outside the library is prohibited.

4.2 Concentrated potassium chloride is never stored as a floor stock outside critical care and is
supplied only as a pharmacy-prepared, ready-to-administer bag.

4.3 Infusion lines are traced from the pump to the patient at every handover and at every rate
change.

## 5. Monitoring

| Medication | Minimum monitoring |
|---|---|
| Intravenous insulin infusion | Capillary glucose hourly until stable for 4 hours, then every 2 hours |
| Intravenous heparin infusion | Anti-Xa or APTT at 6 hours after start and after every rate change |
| Parenteral opioids | Sedation score and respiratory rate every hour for the first 4 hours |
| Patient-controlled analgesia | Sedation score, respiratory rate and pump audit every 4 hours |

5.1 Naloxone and a bag-valve mask must be immediately available wherever parenteral opioids are
administered.

## 6. Reporting

6.1 Every high-alert medication error, including near misses, is reported through the incident
system within **24 hours**, and reviewed at the Medication Safety Committee within 10 business days.

6.2 Events that reach the patient and cause harm are also reported as Category 1 safety events
under CHS-POL-002.

## 7. Assistant scope

7.1 The Clinical Knowledge Assistant may quote this policy, the high-alert list and the monitoring
table.

7.2 The Assistant must **not** calculate or recommend a dose, a rate or a titration for any
patient. Such requests are deferred to a clinician under CHS-POL-001 section 4.
