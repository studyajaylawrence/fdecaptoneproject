## 1. Discharge readiness

1.1 Discharge planning begins within **24 hours of admission** and is reviewed daily at the
interdisciplinary board round.

1.2 A patient is ready for discharge when the discharge criteria recorded on admission are met,
transport is arranged, and the responsible clinician has documented the decision.

## 2. Medication reconciliation

2.1 Medication reconciliation is completed **before** the discharge summary is signed, comparing the
pre-admission list, the inpatient list and the intended discharge list.

2.2 Any intentional change is documented with a reason, in language the patient can understand.

2.3 High-risk medication regimens, including anticoagulants, insulin and opioids, receive a
pharmacist discharge counselling session under CHS-POL-003.

## 3. Follow-up appointments

| Patient group | Follow-up interval |
|---|---|
| Heart failure | Within 7 days |
| Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease exacerbation | Within 7 days |
| New or changed anticoagulation | Within 7 days |
| Newly started antihypertensive therapy | Within 4 weeks, per CHS-PATH-011 |
| All other adult medical discharges | Within 14 days |

3.1 The follow-up appointment is booked **before** the patient leaves the unit, and the date, time
and location appear on the discharge instructions.

3.2 Where an appointment cannot be booked before discharge, the care coordination team contacts the
patient within **2 business days** to arrange it, and records the attempt.

## 4. Discharge summary

4.1 The discharge summary is completed and sent to the primary care clinician within **24 hours** of
discharge.

4.2 The summary must contain: reason for admission, significant findings, procedures, discharge
diagnosis, discharge medication list with changes, pending test results, follow-up arrangements, and
the name and contact of the responsible clinician.

4.3 Pending results outstanding at discharge are tracked by the responsible team until resolved, and
the result is communicated to the patient and to primary care.

## 5. Patient education

5.1 Discharge instructions are written at a reading level suitable for the patient, provided in the
patient's preferred language, and confirmed using the **teach-back method**, which is documented.

5.2 Every patient receives warning signs that should prompt return, and a contact number for
questions: the Care Coordination line, extension **4300**, staffed 08:00-20:00 daily.

## 6. Post-discharge follow-up call

6.1 The care coordination team telephones every patient discharged with a high-risk condition within
**48 hours**, and all other medical discharges within **5 business days**.

6.2 The call reviews medication understanding, symptoms, follow-up attendance and social needs, and
escalates concerns under CHS-POL-002.

## 7. Assistant scope

7.1 The Clinical Knowledge Assistant may answer questions about these timeframes, required content
and escalation routes, with citations.

7.2 Questions about whether a **specific** patient is ready for discharge are clinical decisions and
are deferred under CHS-POL-001.
