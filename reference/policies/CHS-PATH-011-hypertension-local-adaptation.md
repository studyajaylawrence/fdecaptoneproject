## 1. Relationship to the national guideline

1.1 Contoso Health System adopts the VA/DoD Hypertension Clinical Practice Guideline (2026) in
full. This document records only the **local adaptations**: formulary choices, clinic workflows and
referral routes.

1.2 Where this document and the VA/DoD guideline differ, the VA/DoD guideline takes precedence for
clinical recommendations, and this document takes precedence for local process, formulary and
referral timing.

## 2. Diagnosis

2.1 A raised office blood pressure must be confirmed with **out-of-office measurement** before
diagnosis: home blood pressure monitoring twice daily for **7 consecutive days**, discarding day
one, or 24-hour ambulatory monitoring where home monitoring is impractical.

2.2 Contoso supplies validated upper-arm home monitors through the Chronic Care Team. Wrist devices
are not accepted for diagnosis.

2.3 Blood pressure measurement technique follows the VA/DoD guideline sidebar on proper
measurement. Readings taken over clothing or without back and arm support are not valid for
diagnosis.

## 3. Local formulary - first line

| Class | Contoso first choice | Typical starting dose |
|---|---|---|
| Thiazide-type diuretic | chlorthalidone | 12.5 mg daily |
| ACE inhibitor | lisinopril | 10 mg daily |
| Angiotensin receptor blocker (if ACE inhibitor not tolerated) | losartan | 50 mg daily |
| Calcium channel blocker | amlodipine | 5 mg daily |

3.1 Beta blockers are **not** first-line for uncomplicated hypertension at Contoso, and are
reserved for a compelling indication such as ischaemic heart disease or heart failure with reduced
ejection fraction.

3.2 ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers must never be prescribed together.

3.3 All dosing decisions for an individual patient are clinical decisions and sit with the
prescriber, not with the Clinical Knowledge Assistant.

## 4. Follow-up cadence

| Situation | Contoso follow-up interval |
|---|---|
| After starting or changing an antihypertensive | 4 weeks |
| Blood pressure at goal, stable, no comorbidity | 6 months |
| Blood pressure at goal with diabetes or chronic kidney disease | 3 months |
| Blood pressure above goal on three or more agents | 2 weeks, with pharmacist review |

4.1 Each follow-up records blood pressure, adherence, side effects, and home readings.

4.2 The Chronic Care Team runs a nurse-led titration clinic under an approved protocol. Titration
outside that protocol requires prescriber sign-off.

## 5. Referral triggers

5.1 Refer to **nephrology** when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, when urine albumin-to-creatinine
ratio exceeds 300 mg/g, or when there is a rapid decline in eGFR.

5.2 Refer to the **resistant hypertension clinic** when blood pressure remains above goal despite
three agents at optimal doses, one of which is a diuretic.

5.3 Refer **urgently, same day**, when blood pressure is at or above 180/120 mmHg with symptoms
suggesting end-organ damage, such as chest pain, breathlessness, neurological deficit or visual
loss. Follow CHS-POL-002 Tier 3 when the patient is unstable.

5.4 Secondary hypertension screening is arranged for onset before age 30, sudden worsening in a
previously controlled patient, or hypokalaemia without diuretics.

## 6. Documentation

6.1 The diagnosis note must record the confirmatory out-of-office readings, the treatment target
used, and the shared decision-making discussion.

6.2 Home readings are entered into the chronic disease flowsheet within **2 business days** of
receipt by the Chronic Care Team.
