## 1. Screening

1.1 Every adult presenting to the emergency department is screened for sepsis within **15 minutes
of triage**, and re-screened at least every **8 hours** while admitted.

1.2 The screen is positive when a patient has a suspected or confirmed infection **plus two or
more** of:

- respiratory rate >= 22 breaths per minute
- systolic blood pressure <= 100 mmHg
- altered mental state (any new confusion, or GCS < 15)
- heart rate >= 90 beats per minute
- temperature < 36.0 C or > 38.3 C

1.3 A positive screen requires the bedside nurse to notify the treating clinician **immediately**
and to call the Rapid Response Team on extension 2255 if the patient also has systolic blood
pressure below 90 mmHg or a lactate above 4.0 mmol/L.

## 2. The first hour ("Contoso Sepsis Hour")

Clock start (time zero) is the time of the first positive screen or the first clinician
documentation of suspected sepsis, whichever is earlier. Within **60 minutes of time zero**:

| Step | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Measure serum lactate |
| 2 | Obtain blood cultures **before** antimicrobials, provided this does not delay them beyond 60 minutes |
| 3 | Administer broad-spectrum antimicrobials per the Contoso empirical antimicrobial table |
| 4 | Begin 30 mL/kg balanced crystalloid for hypotension or lactate >= 4.0 mmol/L |
| 5 | Apply vasopressors if mean arterial pressure remains below 65 mmHg after fluid resuscitation |
| 6 | Document the sepsis bundle in the sepsis navigator |

## 3. Reassessment

3.1 Repeat serum lactate within **2 to 4 hours** whenever the initial lactate exceeded 2.0 mmol/L,
and continue until it normalises.

3.2 Reassess volume status and tissue perfusion within **one hour** of completing the fluid bolus,
documenting capillary refill, mental state, urine output and mean arterial pressure.

3.3 Escalate to critical care when, after initial resuscitation, the patient needs vasopressors,
has a lactate above 4.0 mmol/L, or has a new oxygen requirement above 6 L/min.

## 4. Antimicrobial stewardship

4.1 The antimicrobial regimen is reviewed by the treating team at **48 hours** and de-escalated
against culture results, and again at **72 hours** with the stewardship pharmacist.

4.2 Intravenous-to-oral switch is considered once the patient has been afebrile for 48 hours, is
haemodynamically stable, and is tolerating oral intake.

4.3 Prolonged courses beyond **7 days** require documented justification and stewardship approval.

## 5. Handover and documentation

5.1 Sepsis status, time zero, bundle completion times and the antimicrobial review date are handed
over at every shift change using the SBAR format.

5.2 Bundle compliance is audited monthly. Sites report bundle completion within 60 minutes, with a
target of **90 percent**.

## 6. Scope limits

6.1 This pathway does not apply to patients under 18 years, to obstetric patients, or to patients
on a documented comfort-focused plan of care. Those patients follow CHS-PATH-014, CHS-PATH-016 and
CHS-POL-009 respectively.

6.2 The Clinical Knowledge Assistant may explain this pathway, but any decision about an individual
patient's antimicrobials, fluids or escalation is a clinical decision and must be referred to a
clinician under CHS-POL-001.
